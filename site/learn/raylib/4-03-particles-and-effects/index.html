Particles and Effects

lesson 4.033 of 5 in milestone 45 min1 practice task

Dust, sparks, smoke and explosions are particles - many small, short-lived objects. This lesson builds a particle system with a fixed pool, emitters with randomised properties, values that change over a particle's life, and additive blending, plus the small effects that make a game feel alive.

What a particle is

A particle is a tiny object with a position, a velocity, a lifetime and a look. Hundreds of them, each living a second or less, make dust when landing, sparks when hitting, smoke from a chimney or an explosion.

C++
struct Particle
{
    Vector2 pos, vel;
    float life, maxLife;      // seconds left, and at birth
    float size;
    Color color;
};

Each frame, every living particle moves (often with gravity or drag), its life goes down by dt, and it is drawn. When life reaches 0 it is dead.

A pool, not new and delete

Creating and destroying hundreds of objects per second with new, or push_back and erase on a vector, wastes time. Use a fixed pool:

  • A std::vector<Particle> with a fixed capacity, such as 2000.
  • An integer count of living particles, all at the front.
  • Spawning writes into slot count and increments it (if the pool is full, either skip or overwrite the oldest).
  • Killing particle i copies the last living particle into slot i and decrements count ("swap and pop"); order does not matter for particles.

No memory is allocated after start-up, and updating walks one tight array.

Emitters

An emitter creates particles with randomised properties:

C++
void burst(Pool& pool, Vector2 at, int n)
{
    for (int i = 0; i < n; ++i)
    {
        const float angle = GetRandomValue(0, 359) * DEG2RAD;
        const float speed = GetRandomValue(60, 180);
        pool.spawn({ at, { std::cos(angle) * speed, std::sin(angle) * speed }, 0.6f, 0.6f, 4.0f, ORANGE });
    }
}

A burst emits many at once (explosion, landing dust). A continuous emitter emits a rate per second, carrying the fraction over to the next frame like the shooter in lesson 1.04, so 30 particles per second is exactly 30 at any frame rate.

Changing over a lifetime

Particles look much better when their properties change as they age. With t = 1 - life / maxLife going from 0 at birth to 1 at death:

Property Typical change
Size Grows (smoke) or shrinks (sparks): lerp(startSize, endSize, t)
Colour Yellow to red to grey: interpolate between colour stops
Alpha Fades out near the end: alpha = 1 - t, or 1 - t² to stay solid longer
Speed Slows with drag

Blending

Draw fire, sparks, magic and lights with additive blending: colours add up, so overlapping particles glow brighter instead of covering each other:

C++
BeginBlendMode(BLEND_ADDITIVE);
for (const Particle& p : pool.alive()) DrawCircleV(p.pos, p.size, p.color);
EndBlendMode();

Smoke and dust use normal (alpha) blending, which darkens and covers.

Small effects with a big impact

  • Hit flash: draw the sprite white for 0.05 s when hit (tint, lesson 2.01).
  • Hit stop: freeze the game for 2 or 3 frames on a heavy hit.
  • Squash and stretch: scale the sprite a little on jumping and landing.
  • Screen shake (lesson 4.01).
  • Floating numbers: damage values that rise and fade, which are particles with text.

These are often called juice: none of them change the rules, all of them change how the game feels.

The First Electronic Computers

lesson 1.033 of 5 in milestone 16 min1 practice task

Between 1936 and 1949 the modern computer was invented - Turing's idea of a universal machine, Zuse's relay computer, Colossus and ENIAC with their thousands of vacuum tubes, and the stored-program design that every computer since has used.

Turing's universal machine (1936)

Before anyone built an electronic computer, Alan Turing worked out what any computer could and could not do. In a 1936 paper he imagined a very simple machine:

  • an endless tape divided into squares, each holding one symbol;
  • a head that reads one square, writes a symbol, and moves left or right;
  • a small table of rules: "in state 3, if you read a 1, write 0, move right, go to state 5".

He showed that this simple machine can calculate anything that can be calculated at all, and that one such machine - a universal machine - can read the rules of any other machine from its tape and imitate it. That is the idea of a computer that runs any program: the program is just more data on the tape.

Every computer since, including your phone, is "Turing-complete": given enough memory and time, it can compute anything any other computer can. They differ in speed, not in what is possible.

Relays, tubes and wartime

The first working computers were built during the Second World War, from the switches available then:

Switch How it works Speed
Relay An electromagnet pulls a metal contact closed. It clicks About 10 switches a second
Vacuum tube (valve) A glowing glass tube; a voltage on a grid lets current through or not Thousands a second, but hot, large and they burned out
Transistor (from 1947) A tiny piece of silicon, no moving parts Billions a second today
Year Computer Who and where What was new
1941 Z3 Konrad Zuse, Berlin The first working programmable, fully automatic computer. Built from about 2,600 relays, and it already used binary
1943-44 Colossus Tommy Flowers, Bletchley Park, UK Electronic (vacuum tubes), built to break German codes. Kept secret until the 1970s
1945 ENIAC Eckert and Mauchly, University of Pennsylvania About 18,000 vacuum tubes, 27 tonnes, a whole room. Used decimal, not binary. Programmed by rewiring cables and setting switches, which took days

The first people to program ENIAC were six women - Kay McNulty, Betty Jennings, Betty Snyder, Marlyn Wescoff, Fran Bilas and Ruth Lichterman - who worked out how to set up its cables and switches from the circuit diagrams, with no manual and no programming language.

The stored-program idea

Rewiring a machine for every new problem was slow. In 1945 John von Neumann wrote a report describing a better design (building on Eckert and Mauchly's work): keep the program in the same memory as the data. Then loading a new program is as quick as reading new numbers, and a program can even work on other programs.

This is called the von Neumann architecture, and nearly every computer since works this way. The CPU fetches an instruction from memory, carries it out, then fetches the next one. You will step through this cycle yourself in lesson 4.04.

Year Computer Why it matters
1948 Manchester Baby (UK) The first computer to run a program stored in its own electronic memory, on 21 June 1948
1949 EDSAC (Cambridge, UK) The first practical stored-program computer in regular use. Its "initial orders" let people write instructions as letters and numbers instead of raw binary: an early step towards assembly language (lesson 5.01)
The first "bug"

In 1947 the team working on the Harvard Mark II relay computer found a moth stuck in a relay, and taped it into their logbook as the "first actual case of bug being found". The word "bug" for a fault was older than that, but the moth made the story famous. Grace Hopper, who told the story often, comes back in lesson 5.02.

How big, how fast

ENIAC could do about 5,000 additions a second. A phone today does tens of billions of operations a second, uses a few watts instead of 150 kilowatts, and fits in a pocket. The ideas, though - binary, stored programs, fetch and execute - are the ones from these few years.

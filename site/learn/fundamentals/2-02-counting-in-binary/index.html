Counting in Binary

lesson 2.022 of 6 in milestone 26 min1 practice task

Binary works exactly like decimal, but each place is worth twice the one to its right instead of ten times. Read a binary number by adding up the places that hold a 1; write a number in binary by repeatedly dividing by 2 and keeping the remainders.

Decimal, slowly

You already know a place-value system. In decimal (base 10), each place is worth ten times the one to its right:

Place thousands hundreds tens units
Worth 1000 100 10 1
Digit of 4,072 4 0 7 2

4,072 means 4 × 1000 + 0 × 100 + 7 × 10 + 2 × 1. There are ten digits, 0 to 9, because a place counts up to 9 and then carries into the next one.

Binary: each place is worth double

Binary (base 2) has only two digits, 0 and 1, and each place is worth twice the one to its right:

Place worth 128 64 32 16 8 4 2 1
Bits of 77 0 1 0 0 1 1 0 1

To read a binary number, add up the place values that have a 1: 01001101 is 64 + 8 + 4 + 1 = 77.

Counting up

Counting in binary is counting in decimal with fewer digits: when a place passes its last digit (1 instead of 9), it goes back to 0 and carries one into the next place.

Decimal Binary Decimal Binary
0 0 8 1000
1 1 9 1001
2 10 10 1010
3 11 11 1011
4 100 12 1100
5 101 13 1101
6 110 14 1110
7 111 15 1111

Notice the patterns: the rightmost bit flips every step (odd numbers end in 1), the next every two steps, the next every four. And 10 in binary is two, 100 is four, 1000 is eight: a 1 followed by zeros is always a power of 2, just as 1000 in decimal is a power of 10.

"10" is not always ten

When a number could be in either base, programmers mark binary with 0b: 0b10 is two, 10 is ten. Python, C, C++, Rust, Java and JavaScript all understand 0b1010.

Decimal to binary: divide by 2

To write 77 in binary, keep dividing by 2. Each remainder is the next bit, from the right:

Step Divide Result Remainder (bit)
1 77 ÷ 2 38 1
2 38 ÷ 2 19 0
3 19 ÷ 2 9 1
4 9 ÷ 2 4 1
5 4 ÷ 2 2 0
6 2 ÷ 2 1 0
7 1 ÷ 2 0 1

Read the remainders from the bottom up: 1001101. That is 77. (With 8 bits, put a 0 in front: 01001101.)

Why it works: dividing by 2 shifts the number one place to the right, and the remainder is the bit that fell off the right end.

In Python, n % 2 is the remainder and n // 2 the whole-number result, so the method is a short loop. You will write it in this lesson's practice.

Binary to decimal: double and add

Going the other way, there is a trick that avoids working out place values: start at 0, and for each bit from the left, double what you have and add the bit.

1001101: 0 → 1 → 2 → 4 → 9 → 19 → 38 → 77.

(0 × 2 + 1 = 1, 1 × 2 + 0 = 2, 2 × 2 + 0 = 4, 4 × 2 + 1 = 9, 9 × 2 + 1 = 19, 19 × 2 + 0 = 38, 38 × 2 + 1 = 77.)

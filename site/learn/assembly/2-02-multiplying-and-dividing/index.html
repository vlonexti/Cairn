Multiplying and Dividing

lesson 2.022 of 5 in milestone 25 min1 practice task

imul multiplies with two or three operands and keeps the low bits of the answer. div and idiv divide the 128-bit number in rdx:rax by an operand, leaving the quotient in rax and the remainder in rdx - so rdx must be prepared first, with xor for unsigned and cqo for signed. Dividing by zero stops the program.

imul

Form Does
imul rax, rbx rax = rax × rbx
imul rax, rbx, 10 rax = rbx × 10
imul rcx, [rel price] rcx = rcx × the 8 bytes at price

The answer of a 64 × 64-bit multiplication can need 128 bits; these forms keep the low 64 and drop the rest. For signed and unsigned numbers the low bits are the same, so imul serves both.

Assembly
        global  _start
        section .text
_start:
        mov     rax, 12
        imul    rax, rax        ; 144
        imul    rbx, rax, 3     ; rbx = 432
        mov     rdi, rbx
        call    print_int
        call    print_newline
        mov     eax, 60
        xor     edi, edi
        syscall

The old one-operand forms, mul rbx (unsigned) and imul rbx (signed), multiply rax by the operand and put the full 128-bit answer in rdx:rax: the high 64 bits in rdx, the low 64 in rax. You need them when the high half matters.

Division: rdx:rax

Division is the awkward one. div rbx divides the 128-bit number made of rdx (high half) and rax (low half) by rbx:

  • the quotient goes in rax,
  • the remainder in rdx.

So before dividing, rdx must hold the right high half:

Numbers Before Divide
unsigned xor edx, edx (high half 0) div rbx
signed cqo (copies rax's sign bit into all of rdx) idiv rbx
Assembly
        global  _start
        section .text
_start:
        mov     rax, 17
        xor     edx, edx        ; unsigned: rdx:rax = 17
        mov     ecx, 5
        div     rcx             ; rax = 3, rdx = 2
        mov     rbx, rdx        ; keep the remainder
        mov     rdi, rax
        call    print_int
        call    print_newline
        mov     rdi, rbx
        call    print_int
        call    print_newline

        mov     rax, -17
        cqo                     ; signed: rdx = all 1s, because -17 is negative
        mov     ecx, 5
        idiv    rcx             ; rax = -3, rdx = -2 (rounds towards zero, like C)
        mov     rbx, rdx
        mov     rdi, rax
        call    print_int
        call    print_newline
        mov     rdi, rbx
        call    print_int
        call    print_newline
        mov     eax, 60
        xor     edi, edi
        syscall
Forgetting rdx

If rdx still holds something from earlier, div divides a huge 128-bit number, and when the quotient does not fit in 64 bits the processor raises a divide error: the program stops with exit code 136, "Floating point exception" (an old name: it is an integer problem). Always set rdx right before div, and remember that div also overwrites rdx.

Dividing by zero does the same. The divisor cannot be a constant (div 10 does not exist): put it in a register first.

Dividing by constants without div

div is slow (around 20 to 40 cycles, against 3 for imul), so compilers avoid it:

  • Dividing an unsigned number by a power of two is a right shift: shr rax, 3 divides by 8 (lesson 2.03).
  • Dividing by other constants becomes a multiplication by a "magic" reciprocal, then a shift. You will see code like imul rax, rax, 1431655766 in compiler output and now know why (lesson 6.01).

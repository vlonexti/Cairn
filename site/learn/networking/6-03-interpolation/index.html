Interpolation

lesson 6.033 of 5 in milestone 65 min1 practice task

Making other players move smoothly from snapshots that arrive 20 to 60 times a second with jitter and loss - rendering a little in the past, blending between the two snapshots around the render time, the size of the delay, and limited extrapolation.

The problem

Snapshots arrive 20 to 60 times a second, unevenly (jitter), and some are lost. If a client simply placed every other player at the position from the latest snapshot, they would jump from point to point, stutter when a snapshot arrived late, and freeze when one was lost.

Render in the past

The standard answer is entity interpolation: show other entities slightly in the past, at renderTime = now - delay, and blend between the two snapshots on either side of that time.

Text
snapshots:   t=100      t=150      t=200      t=250
                              ^ renderTime = 175
position = snapshot150 + (snapshot200 - snapshot150) · (175 - 150) / (200 - 150)

Because the render time is behind the newest snapshot, the client usually already holds the snapshot after it, so it can always blend smoothly. The delay is chosen to cover the gap between snapshots plus some jitter, typically two snapshot intervals: about 100 ms at 20 snapshots a second (Counter-Strike's long-standing default), less at higher rates. If one snapshot is lost, the client blends across the wider gap and nobody notices.

The cost is that other players are seen where they were about 100 ms ago, plus half the round trip. That is why the next two lessons exist: prediction for your own player, lag compensation for hitting others.

Times and buffers

  • Use the server's tick numbers (or times derived from them), not the time each snapshot happened to arrive: arrival times include jitter, which is exactly what interpolation is meant to hide.
  • Keep a short buffer of recent snapshots per entity, sorted by time, and drop ones older than the render time needs.
  • Ignore a snapshot older than the newest one held: it arrived out of order and adds nothing.
  • Clients keep their idea of "now in server ticks" by tracking the latest snapshot tick and advancing it with their own clock, nudging it gently when snapshots arrive earlier or later than expected.

When snapshots stop

If the render time passes the newest snapshot (several are lost in a row), there is nothing to blend towards. Options:

  • Hold the last position: safe, but the entity freezes.
  • Extrapolate from the last two snapshots' velocity for a short time (dead reckoning), then hold. A little extrapolation hides a short gap; a lot shows players running into walls and snapping back when the truth arrives. Limit it to about one snapshot interval.

Interpolate other values the same way: angles (taking the short way round), animation times. Discrete values (alive or dead, which weapon) simply switch when the render time passes their snapshot.

Ethernet and IP Headers

lesson 4.022 of 5 in milestone 45 min1 practice task

Decoding the first layers of a captured packet - the Ethernet frame with MAC addresses and EtherType, the IPv4 header field by field with its options and checksum, fragments, and the fixed 40-byte IPv6 header.

Ethernet

A captured Ethernet frame starts with a 14-byte header:

Offset Size Field
0 6 destination MAC address
6 6 source MAC address
12 2 EtherType: what follows

MAC addresses are written as six hex bytes separated by colons: 3c:22:fb:12:9a:01. The EtherType values you will meet:

EtherType Protocol
0x0800 IPv4
0x86DD IPv6
0x0806 ARP (finding the MAC address for an IP on the local network)
0x8100 a VLAN tag: 4 more bytes, then the real EtherType

(The frame check sequence at the end of the frame is usually not included in captures.)

IPv4

The IPv4 header follows at offset 14:

Offset Size Field
0 4 bits version: 4
0 4 bits IHL: header length in 32-bit words (5 = 20 bytes, more if options)
1 1 DSCP and ECN (priority and congestion marking)
2 2 total length: header plus data, in bytes
4 2 identification (shared by the fragments of one packet)
6 3 bits + 13 bits flags (bit 1: don't fragment, bit 2: more fragments) and fragment offset in 8-byte units
8 1 TTL
9 1 protocol: 1 ICMP, 6 TCP, 17 UDP
10 2 header checksum
12 4 source address
16 4 destination address
20 ... options, if IHL > 5

All big-endian. Decoding rules:

  • The transport header starts at IHL × 4 bytes, not always 20.
  • Use the total length to know where the IP data ends: Ethernet pads small frames to 60 bytes, and those padding bytes are not part of the packet.
  • The checksum covers the header only (lesson 2.03's algorithm over IHL × 4 bytes): a correct header sums to zero.
  • A packet with the "more fragments" flag or a non-zero fragment offset is a fragment: only the first fragment carries the TCP or UDP header; the others carry continuation data. A decoder that treats every fragment as starting with a TCP header reads garbage.

IPv6

IPv6 has a fixed 40-byte header, simpler to parse:

Offset Size Field
0 4 bits version: 6
0 8 bits + 20 bits traffic class and flow label
4 2 payload length (everything after the 40-byte header)
6 1 next header: 6 TCP, 17 UDP, 58 ICMPv6, or an extension header
7 1 hop limit (IPv6's TTL)
8 16 source address
24 16 destination address

There is no header checksum (the layers above have their own), and optional information comes as a chain of extension headers, each naming the next, which a full decoder must walk before it reaches TCP or UDP.

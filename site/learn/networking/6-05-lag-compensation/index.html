Lag Compensation

lesson 6.055 of 5 in milestone 65 min1 practice task

Making hits fair when every player sees others in the past - the server keeping a short history of positions, rewinding targets to the moment the shooter saw, testing the shot there, limiting the rewind, and the trade-offs it creates for the player being shot.

Aiming at the past

Thanks to interpolation (lesson 6.03), a client sees other players where they were about 100 ms ago, plus the time its own input takes to reach the server. A player who aims perfectly at a running enemy on their screen and fires would miss if the server tested the shot against where the enemy is now: by the time the shot arrives, the enemy has moved on. Players would have to aim ahead by an amount that changes with their ping. Nobody wants that.

Rewinding the world

Lag compensation makes the server test the shot against what the shooter actually saw:

  1. The server keeps a history of every player's position (and hitbox) for each of the last few hundred milliseconds of ticks.
  2. The fire input says which tick the shooter was seeing: the render time of its interpolation, as a tick number (it can be fractional, between two snapshots).
  3. The server rewinds the other players to that tick, interpolating between the two recorded ticks around it, just as the client did.
  4. It tests the shot (a ray, the bullet's path) against those rewound positions, then puts everything back.

If the shooter's screen showed a hit, the server agrees. The shooter's own position comes from the present, since that is known exactly.

Limits

The client says which tick it saw, so the server must not trust it blindly:

  • Clamp the rewind to a maximum, typically 200 ms to 1 second. A client claiming to see the world 3 seconds ago gets the oldest allowed tick instead, so a laggy or dishonest client cannot rewrite history far back.
  • Reject ticks in the future (newer than the server's current tick).
  • Check the claimed tick against what the server knows about that client's latency; a value that jumps around is suspicious.

The trade-off

Lag compensation favours the shooter. The player being shot sometimes feels it is unfair: they reached cover on their screen, then died anyway, because on the shooter's screen (100 ms behind) they had not yet. This "shot around the corner" effect grows with the shooter's latency, which is the main reason for capping the rewind. Every shooter game tunes this balance; some reduce compensation for players with very high ping.

Lag compensation is for instant hits (hitscan weapons). Slow projectiles, such as rockets and arrows, are usually simulated on the server from the moment they are fired, sometimes with a small head start to make up for the shooter's latency.
